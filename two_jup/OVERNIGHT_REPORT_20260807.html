Overnight autonomous session · 146 / 148 · R3 f1536

The modem is fine. Our measurements were not — and an ARQ was never switched on.

7 Aug 2026, 00:10–06:25 EDT · board 146 only · 148 untouched

The night's main result is a stop, not a fix. Timing-loop Bn ×2 — on the dashboard as a confirmed 8× win and heading toward being landed as a constant — is catastrophic under real traffic. Delivered PER 92.4% versus 1.50% control.

ARQ-off PER is ~1.5% tonight and fails the gate, but that is the channel, not a code regression: the exact image the 0.283% gate was taken on measures the same ~1.4% right now.

The one real win: an ARQ that was never switched on. With it enabled the typical run measures 0.03–0.10% — twenty to sixty times better than any ARQ-off run. But roughly one run in three fails to engage and lands at ~2%, so a 3-run gate passes or fails on which runs it draws: two identical sets 25 minutes apart gave 0.661% (pass) and 1.018% (fail). The gate is not reproducibly met — but I then caught the failure in the act: the bad runs are the runs where the peer receives zero NAKs, so nothing is ever retransmitted. That's a delivery/startup bug, not a tuning problem, and it's the highest-value target left.

No fabric change was made. 146 is back on the combined T9.0 image, link up, bring-up at its committed defaults.

Withdrawn

Two measurement defects, both of which had reached reported numbers

Reset-truncated windows. 0x104 is reset by rstCS and by the watchdog's 0x000 re-arm. Our rate primitive guards only against negative deltas, so a reset inside the window yields a positive-but-far-too-small value. Resets every ~15 s over a 30 s window read 622 f/s on a link actually running 1244.

Shared register latch. stallpoll and lock_watchdog both used the one direct_reg_access address latch, so reads returned foreign registers — 0xC010180 (which is adc_forensic 0x15C) appeared 68 times in the soak CSVs' packet-count column. Rare at 0.09% of rows, but one such value inflates a run delta by 2×10⁸.

Withdrawn as a result: the "link spends 58% / 70% / 85% of its time degraded" figures I derived earlier tonight. All three came through the contaminated path, and their spread under different filtering was itself the tell. New primitive rate_probe.sh samples across the dwell and reports rate, resets/s and valid-% as independent columns.

Closed · known class

The idle collapse is the documented host-wedge, already handled

On an idle link the rate runs at nominal for 5–20 s after every arm, then steps to ~40% and stays — with zero counter resets across 120 s, so this is not the artifact above.

  • One direction only. Across all 30 gate probes tonight, 148 RX was full rate 30/30 (median 1241); 146 RX 11/30 (median 882).
  • Not carrier sync. An 0x110 rstCS pulse does nothing; an 0x000 soft re-arm restores full rate for ~20 s, then it steps again.
  • Timing loop implicated. 0x184 strobe_forensic — the Symbol Synchronizer's max inter-strobe gap — latches 5→6 exactly at the step. The T8.5 P-path and integrator upset counters read 0 throughout, so this is not the Delay14 class the TMR fix addresses.

It is a named, already-handled failure class. Thirty minutes of the watchdog's own samples on an idle 146: 118 LOCKED windows, 83 NOT-LOCKED, 41 full re-arms — about one every 44 s. All 83 NOT-LOCKED windows are HOST-WEDGE detections, and their packet counts sit in a tight band (min 2325, median 2509, max 2595 per 5 s ≈ 500 f/s) — exactly the step-down rate. During the step-down the fabric still syncs ~500 frames/s while the host decodes nothing: idle_rx + dma_rx_ok frozen. That is precisely the sync-but-CRC-fail wedge lock_watchdog was written to catch.

That closes the mechanism too. rstCS has no effect while an 0x000 soft re-arm restores full rate for ~20 s — which is the watchdog's recovery for this class. My traces ran with the watchdog killed, so the wedge persisted 110–160 s once entered; with it running it clears every ~44 s. Same phenomenon, different persistence.

So this needs no new work: known class, already detected and recovered in normal operation, and it does not happen under traffic (1241 f/s flat at 94% CRC-OK for 60 s). Worth noting only that an idle link churns through a re-arm every ~44 s for no benefit.

I briefly wrote the opposite — that the step-down might be an artifact of my having killed the watchdog — on the strength of 11 windows reading full rate. That was a lucky stretch; across 118 windows the wedge state occupies ~32%. Withdrawn. I over-corrected on n=11 immediately after spending the night warning myself about single-sample conclusions.

Null result

16 arms with working instrumentation: FULL 6 (38%), 0.71× 1, 0.42× 9 (56%) — matching the historical 37/5/53, so the lottery is stable across images and days. Confirmed · act on this

The cross-link ARQ has never been switched on

In two-radio -G mode qpsk_tun sets arq_on = 0 by design — the board that detects a loss is not the board that sent the frame — and bringup_r2r3.sh has never passed -A, the flag that enables the cross-link NAK ARQ written for exactly this topology. On the rig before the change: retx=0 dups=0 recovered=0 naks_tx=0 across 93,936 received frames, with seq_gap=662. Gaps were being detected and nothing was ever retransmitted.

That is also why the loss budget below closes with zero net ARQ recovery.

Six complete interleaved pairs, analysed at the run level: mean paired improvement +1.07 points, median +1.19, 5 of 6 pairs favour ARQ, paired t = +3.15 on 5 df. Drop the largest pair and the mean is still +0.82 — the result does not hinge on one run. 4 of 6 ARQ-on runs are under 1%; 0 of 6 ARQ-off runs are. Wedge drops were symmetric (one per arm), so attrition isn't biasing the survivors.

The two sets differ by more than noise comfortably explains — the off arm went 1.566% → 1.874% while the on arm went 1.061% → 0.243%, a recovery fraction of 32% then 87%. I had written this up as "worse channel, more to recover," but that is backwards: more loss predicts a bigger delta, not a better absolute on-arm value, and the counter analysis predicts the opposite anyway, since a hole-table-limited ARQ should recover a smaller fraction under heavier load. Two things changed between the sets — the channel and the daemon binary (the ARQ constants became runtime variables). The banner confirms the parameter values, not that the change left the per-frame hole scan untouched under -O2. The pooled direction is what's supported; the per-set mechanism is not established.

On attrition: equal drop counts are not unbiased attrition. Pair 4's dropped ARQ-on run measured 1.754% — the worst ARQ-on run of the night — excluded only because its partner wedged. Impute it and the mean softens from +1.07 to ≈+0.92, sign test 5/7. The conclusion survives; the number moves.

I first reported this from the initial three pairs as "real, non-overlapping CP95 intervals on ~170k frames per arm." That argument was wrong — Clopper-Pearson on pooled frames assumes independent trials and these losses are bursty, so the effective n was 3, not 170,000. The direction survived, but the fix was collecting more pairs, not better statistics on the same three.

Why it under-delivers, from the counters: recovered=464 against naks_tx=388 and arq_lost=3631. Only ~11% of holes are ever recovered, and every NAK control frame consumes a real tx_seq that is never delivered — so the two nearly cancel. Mistuned, not broken. Tuning targets are AXR_NAK_TRIES, AXR_RENAK, AXR_HOLE_SZ, and making NAK frames not consume a delivered seq. This is the highest-value PER work I can identify from tonight.

An earlier non-interleaved set measured pooled 0.675% and a gate pass; interleaving showed much of that was the channel improving over those 18 minutes. That claim is superseded.

I tried the tuning and it was inconclusive. The four parameters are now runtime-settable (QPSK_ARQ_HOLES/TRIES/RENAK/CLAMP, verified on hardware as default-identical when unset). An aggressive first step — 4× table, 8 NAK tries — gave 0.034%, 8.712%, 8.155% against a stock arm where two of three captures wedged outright. The link deteriorated across those 35 minutes, so the arms aren't comparable. Do not read the 0.034% as a win; the same arm produced 8.7% half an hour later. If anything the two 8% runs are a caution that 8 tries per hole buys a lot of NAK air time. Re-test on a stable channel with a smaller step.

So the deliverable is not "ARQ meets the gate." It is that ARQ makes the typical run beat the gate by ~50×, and the remaining work is the ~1-in-3 engagement failure — which I then caught in the act. See below.

Root cause found

The bad runs are the runs where NAKs never reach the peer

Four ARQ-on captures with the ARQ counters snapshotted on both ends after each run, lined up against that run's PER. The chain is 146.seq_gap → 146.naks_tx → 148.naks_rx → 148.retx → 146.recovered.

The bad run is exactly the run where the chain is severed, at one identifiable link: 146 sent 1462 NAKs and 148 received zero — not few, zero — so nothing was retransmitted and nothing recovered. Every run where 148 saw NAKs recovered hundreds of frames and landed at 0.02–0.89%.

All of 148's counters read zero that run, including seq_gap, so the likely reading is that its ARQ layer wasn't processing the reverse stream at all — a late daemon start relative to 146, or frames not reaching the ARQ layer — rather than NAKs being lost on air, which would give a small naks_rx rather than 0.

This is a NAK-delivery/startup failure, not a tuning problem — a better place to be than the hole-table tuning I was chasing at 03:00. The hole-table numbers look similar on good and bad runs, so they are not what separates them, and tuning AXR_* would not have fixed this. Next step is one counter on the AXR_MAGIC check on 148, which separates "no NAK frames arrived" from "arrived but not parsed" in a single run, plus a look at daemon start ordering in bring-up.

Loss budget

Where the 1.4% actually comes from

Steady-state (t≥15 s) raw CRC failure is 0.95–1.09%, and it is almost entirely isolated single frames — 334 of 397 bursts are one frame long, and bursts over 20 frames carry only 5–6% of the losses.

The full-window figure looks like 5.9%, but 85% of that sits in exactly three ~1.0 s bursts per run at identical absolute times across independent runs (7.9 / 9.8 / 11.6 s, spaced 1.83 s). That is the one-time RF-enable warmup cluster, and acceptance_rxq.sh already excludes it. It contaminates no gate number.

Delivered PER exceeded steady-state CRC failure, which is impossible if ARQ only ever helps — that gap is what led to the ARQ finding above. The remainder is frames never detected at all: a framesync miss produces no host record and no 0x104 advance, so it is invisible to crc_ok but shows as a missing host_seq. It is measurable as the shortfall against nominal geometry, and the budget then closes per run to within 0.02 points:

Corrections to my own earlier claims

Three times tonight a result inverted when I changed the measurement rather than the treatment: degraded-arm confound → reset artifact → idle-vs-traffic. The common failure was validating a treatment against a control I had not verified was in the intended regime.

The metric that survived every challenge is the per-frame log under real saturating traffic. Fabric register deltas on an idle link is the one that kept lying. Also withdrawn: an 8-arm claim that strobeAlignError never coincides with a full-rate arm — arms 11 and 12 broke it. I flagged it as weak and waited for full n, which was the right call.

And one retracted inside the hour: I derived "host-missed frames 10–12%" from reg_packets jumps, then found it fails its own sanity check — net fabric advance 56,477 against 56,473 host records, a difference of four frames rather than 5,852. The host logs in DMA batches and samples a free-running counter at log time. Host-missed frames are ~0; the metric is unusable.

One tooling bug worth knowing: acceptance_rxq.sh's inline analyzer reported two runs as bins={'21-100': 208, '>100': 84} while counting 3 and 5 total misses — internally impossible. accept_analyze.py gives sane bins on the same captures and the PER figures agree, so only the inline histogram is wrong. That same inline analyzer also threw cp_upper math-domain and IndexError crashes on near-zero-miss runs tonight. Don't quote its bins; it needs a pass.

The last correction is the sharpest, because I had already learned the lesson: I refused to read a single 0.034% run as a win in the ARQ tuning test, then two hours earlier had let a single 0.284% run carry the ARQ effect size — laundered through frame-pooling instead of through a monotonic sequence, so it didn't look like the same mistake. Pooling frames across runs hides run-level variance; when losses are bursty, the run is the unit.

What I'd do next

  • Set FWD_GATE=1 as the default in capture_r3.sh. FH=100 is hardcoded and fwd_health only runs when the flag is set, so every "fwd 100%" line in every capture log tonight is a placeholder, not a measurement — only the reverse direction was ever gated. With the flag on I directly observed a capture starting at rev 98% fwd 0%; the gate caught it and retried to 94%. Measuring forward health instead of asserting it is right on its own merits. Whether it also explains the ARQ bimodality is a strong, cheap hypothesis I could not test — the channel degraded too far by 06:20 (one run hit 25% PER at 49% CRC drop) for those runs to be comparable.
  • Close the NAK-delivery failure. Highest-value item left, and now localized: add a counter on 148's AXR_MAGIC check to separate "no NAK frames arrived" from "arrived but not parsed" — one run settles it — then check daemon start ordering between the boards, since a late-starting peer produces exactly the observed all-zero signature. The good runs prove link and ARQ are jointly capable of 0.022%, roughly 50× margin against the gate, so closing this meets the gate structurally instead of by luck.
  • Turn -A on by default anyway. On this evidence it is strictly non-inferior: the typical run is dramatically better and the bad run is no worse than ARQ-off. One flag, no rebuild, reversible. I did not make the change unattended because it alters standing bring-up behaviour for both boards.
  • Then tune it. Its failure modes are legible: stop NAK frames consuming a delivered sequence number, then work AXR_NAK_TRIES / AXR_RENAK / AXR_HOLE_SZ against the ~11% hole-recovery rate. The knobs are already runtime settable. Use a small step — my 4× jump was inconclusive and possibly harmful.
  • Then the packet detector, which owns the ~0.38% never-detected share and is independently implicated by the stall work.
  • Restate the gate with channel conditions attached. ~1.5% is tonight's honest number on both images, and 0.283% is not reproducible on demand.
  • Pin rx0 explicitly and re-run 16 arms — cheap, no rebuild. It won't kill the lottery but it removes a known uncontrolled variable.
  • Don't add a watchdog rate-class check unless the idle collapse turns out to matter operationally. It does not affect traffic-carrying links.
